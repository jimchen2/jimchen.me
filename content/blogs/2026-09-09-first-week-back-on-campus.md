Semester started on Monday, which means my desk has gone from "clean surface I eat lunch on" back to "geological survey of half-finished ideas."

## The first seminar

Our reading group met Wednesday to go over a paper on retrieval for long documents. I had read maybe two thirds of it, which turned out to be exactly the amount needed to ask a question that sounded intelligent and land me with a follow-up reading list. Fair trade.

The part that stuck with me is how much of the evaluation section was about what the authors *chose not to measure*. There is a paragraph, easy to skim past, where they admit their benchmark saturates after a certain corpus size. That honesty is what I want in my own writing this year.

## Small systems, small joys

I finally fixed the little script that syncs my reading notes into this blog's draft folder. It is forty lines of Python and it does one thing: watch a directory, convert Markdown, and drop the result where the site build expects it. I have rewritten it three times over two years. Each version is worse in some ways and better in others, which I have decided to call progress.

The coffee machine in the department kitchen is still broken. Someone taped a note to it that says "temporarily out of service" dated March 2025.

## What I want from this semester

- Finish the survey chapter draft by November, not January.
- Read one non-technical book a month. Currently on a translated novella I keep putting down and picking back up.
- Keep this blog alive with at least one post a week, even when the post is just a journal entry like this one.

That last one is the hard part. Writing publicly is a muscle and mine has atrophied over the summer, when the only thing I published was a photo story from a road trip and three half-finished notes about databases.

## Evening walk

I walked the loop around campus after dinner. Late light, empty quad, a group of freshmen taking photos of a building I have walked past a hundred times without looking at. That is the trick of a new semester: everything is new to someone, and if you pay attention, a little of it rubs off.

Tomorrow: office hours, laundry, and another attempt at the proofs I abandoned on Friday.
