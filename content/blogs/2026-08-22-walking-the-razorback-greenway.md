August in northwest Arkansas arrives fully armed. By nine in the morning the air has weight to it, and the cicadas have started what I can only describe as a sustained argument. So I have taken to walking early.

## The route

Out the door, left down the hill, and onto the greenway where it crosses the road by the library. From there it is a straight, flat, gravel four miles that follows the creek out towards the lake. Flat is a relative term in this part of the state — the Ozarks are never really flat, but by local standards this is a runway.

At six there are maybe a dozen other people out. Two serious runners in matching gear who pass me at the same point every single morning. An older couple walking a beagle that has clearly negotiated a shorter route. A guy with a metal detector, which I have never once seen him use.

## What I noticed this week

The creek is low. You can walk across it in three places now, on rocks that are underwater in April. The herons have moved to the deeper pools near the dam, and they let you get within about ten feet before they decide you are a problem.

There is a stretch where the path runs under the road and the temperature drops five degrees. You can feel it on your forearms. Everyone slows down slightly going through it and nobody talks about it.

I have started bringing a small notebook instead of my phone. It turns out that if your hands are occupied with something analog, you pay attention to different things: the exact colour of the light on the water at 6:40, the way gravel sounds different under running shoes than under sandals.

## Why I keep doing it

Not for the exercise, though it counts. It is that the first hour of the day is the only hour I have that has not been allocated to anything. No meetings, no reading list, no draft with a cursor blinking at the end of an unfinished sentence.

By seven I am back, showered, and eating something with too much hot sauce in it, and the day starts like everyone else's. But I got an hour of it first, and on days when the work is going badly, that hour is the difference between "hard week" and "bad week."

Tomorrow I am taking the long way back through the neighbourhood with the absurd houses. There is one with a full-size lighthouse in the front yard and I have questions.
