One month in, so here is the honest accounting rather than the highlight reel.

## The numbers

| | Week 1 | Week 2 | Week 3 | Week 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minutes per day | 45 | 30 | 25 | 30 |
| New words | 60 | 45 | 30 | 20 |
| Sentences I can say out loud | 5 | 12 | 20 | 35 |

Total: roughly 21 hours. My vocabulary is somewhere around 150 active words, which is enough to be polite and not enough to be interesting.

## Cyrillic is a non-issue

Everyone warns about the alphabet. It took a weekend, mostly because it is a false difficulty — a third of the letters look like Latin ones and mean the same thing, another third look like Latin letters and mean something else entirely, and the rest are new but few.

The ones that will get you are the false friends:

- **В** is `v`, not `b`
- **Н** is `n`, not `h`
- **Р** is `r`, not `p`
- **С** is `s`, not `c`
- **Х** is the `kh` sound, not `x`

After that, reading is just slow rather than impossible. I can read a street sign or a menu now, which feels disproportionate to the effort it took.

## The case system is the real work

Russian has six cases, and the case system is not decoration — it does the job that word order does in English. "The dog bit the man" and "The man bit the dog" can share a word order and differ only in endings.

I have been doing the obvious thing: learn whole phrases rather than declension tables, and let the patterns accumulate. It works for comprehension. It does not yet work for production, because when I am speaking I have to choose an ending in real time and I default to the nominative every time, which apparently sounds like a telegram from 1920.

The prepositions are what surprise me. **в** and **на** both translate as "in" or "on", and which one you use is a property of the noun, not of the geometry. You go **в магазин** but **на почту**. There are patterns. I have not internalised them.

## What actually moved the needle

1. **Reading things I already knew.** A translated children's book I had read in English as a kid. Knowing the plot removes half the cognitive load and leaves capacity for the language.
2. **One tutor call a week.** Thirty minutes of being forced to produce output is worth more than three hours of flashcards for me.
3. **Changing my phone's keyboard.** I type in Russian to my language exchange partner, and typos teach you spelling faster than any exercise.

## What did not

Flashcard apps with no context. Listening to podcasts pitched well above my level, where I retain the rhythm and none of the meaning. And "immersion" as a passive activity — having Russian radio on while I cook has produced exactly zero measurable progress.

## Next month

Same time budget, but shifted: less vocabulary, more speaking. I want to get to the point where I can hold a two-minute conversation about something I did yesterday without switching to English halfway through. That is the real milestone, and it is further away than the vocabulary count suggests.
