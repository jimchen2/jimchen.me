Some version of this question comes up every time I tell someone I maintain my own site. The implied follow-up is always the same: why not just post it where the people are?

## The honest answer

Because the places where the people are optimise for something that is not writing. A feed rewards a first line. A blog rewards a last paragraph. I would rather be good at the second thing.

There is also the plain fact that I have been writing here since middle school, back when the posts were YA book reviews and the CSS was worse. Deleting that continuity to chase a platform that will itself be gone in five years seems like a bad trade.

## What the site is for

Three things, in order of how much I actually care:

**Thinking.** A paragraph that I have only thought is not a paragraph. Writing it down is where I find out whether the idea survives contact with a sentence. Half the posts here started as something I was confident about and ended with me disagreeing with the version of myself that started typing.

**Remembering.** The travel journals are for me at forty, not for anyone else. I cannot reliably recall what a Tuesday felt like three years ago. The blog can.

**A small, weird public.** A handful of people read this. Some of them have emailed me about a post, and two of them I now count as friends. That is a better outcome than ten thousand passive views.

## What I have stopped doing

- Writing for a schedule. When I had a "publish every Tuesday" rule, the Tuesdays that had nothing to say produced the worst things on this site.
- Explaining the obvious to a hypothetical beginner. It flattens everything.
- Chasing analytics. I keep a view counter because it is mildly interesting. I do not look at referrers anymore.

## The technical part, briefly

The site is deliberately boring. Markdown files in a Git repository, a database for the dynamic bits, no build-time JavaScript framework drama beyond what is needed to render a page. Every time I have been tempted to rewrite it, the rewrite has lasted a weekend and the old version has outlived it.

That constraint is a feature. The cost of publishing here is: write a file, push, done. If publishing costs more than fifteen minutes of friction, I will not do it on a Wednesday night when I have something small to say, and the small things are most of what is worth saying.

## A note to whoever finds this later

If you landed here from a search engine looking for the gradient descent post, the other stuff is mostly journals and travel notes. There is a language learning series, some book reviews from when I was thirteen that I have decided not to delete, and far too many posts about walking around cities.

Stay a while. There is no newsletter.
