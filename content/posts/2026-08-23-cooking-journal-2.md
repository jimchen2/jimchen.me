Continuing the cooking journal series. The rule of this series is that I write down what I actually made, including the failures, especially the failures.

## Pork and cabbage dumplings, 60 of them

I finally stopped measuring the filling and started tasting it: fry a tiny patty of the mixture before assembling, season the bowl, not the dumpling. Sixty dumplings is exactly two meals for me and one meal for friends, and folding them while a podcast talks is the cheapest therapy available in Fayetteville.

The dough ratio I settle on, for the record:

1. 300 g middle-gluten flour
2. 150 g cold water
3. A pinch of salt, mostly ceremonial
4. Rest 30 minutes under a damp towel, no shortcuts, the towel is the recipe

Wrappers rolled by hand, uneven, some of them hexagonal. They seal worse than machine-round ones and taste better, which I cannot explain and will not investigate.

## Scallion oil noodles (葱油拌面)

The Shanghai dish I make when I am homesick and lazy, which is my default state. Slow-fry a whole bunch of scallions in neutral oil until they are the color of dark honey, then:

- 2 tbsp of the scallion oil
- 1.5 tbsp light soy sauce
- 0.5 tbsp dark soy sauce, for color
- 1 tsp sugar, non-negotiable, this is a sweet-savory dish
- Boiled noodles, tossed hot, eaten standing at the counter

The scallion crisps go on top. If they burn, start over; burnt scallion oil is bitter in a way no amount of sugar fixes. I learned this the second time, not the first.

## The borscht incident

After my Russia trip I became convinced I could make borscht. I could not. What I made was a beet soup with strong opinions and no balance: too much vinegar early, no sweetness late, and I salted it at the start like a person who has never read a recipe in his life. Beets need their sugar added at the end, and the sour note should arrive last, like a guest, not first, like a landlord.

> A recipe is a letter from someone who already made the mistakes. I keep insisting on reading it as a suggestion.

Next attempt: follow one recipe completely, word for word, even the parts I think are wrong. Especially the parts I think are wrong.
