It rained for six days straight. Not the dramatic Arkansas thunderstorms that flip umbrellas inside out, just a gray, patient drizzle that made every sidewalk on Dickson Street smell like wet concrete and fried food. I barely left the house, and honestly, it was one of the better weeks of my semester.

## The shape of the week

I kept a tiny log because I am trying to be the kind of person who keeps a tiny log:

- **Monday** — Woke up at 11. Read half a paper on KV caching, understood a third of it. Made tea, watched the rain move across the parking lot like a slow curtain.
- **Tuesday** — Office hours, then the lab fridge was empty, so I ate instant noodles over the sink like a raccoon.
- **Wednesday** — Wrote 400 words of the related-work section. Deleted 300 of them. The 100 that survived are good.
- **Thursday** — Called my mom in Shanghai. It was 3 a.m. her next morning and she picked up immediately, which is both comforting and concerning.
- **Friday** — The rain stopped for exactly one hour. I walked to the trailhead behind campus and back. Worth it.
- **Saturday** — Farmers market anyway, damp tomatoes, a busker playing a erhu of all instruments, in Arkansas, in the rain. I stood there for ten minutes like an idiot with an umbrella.

## What I actually think about rain days

There is a version of productivity culture that treats a day indoors as a day owed. I don't believe that anymore. The rain week gave me something I never get from a normal week: **uninterrupted low-stakes time**. No obligation to go anywhere means no background computation about going anywhere. My brain idled, and idling is where the good sentences come from.

> A day of rain is a day the world lowers its expectations of you, and you should accept the gift.

I also noticed I read differently when it rains. On dry days I read with one eye on the door. On wet days I read the way I did in middle school, lying on the floor with my feet up on a chair, which remains the correct posture for reading and I will not be taking questions.

## Small things that helped

1. A desk lamp instead of the ceiling light. The ceiling light makes the room feel like an obligation.
2. Putting the phone in a drawer during the 4 p.m. slump. The slump still happens, but it happens to me, not to my feed.
3. Cooking one real meal a day instead of three assembled meals. Even a bad stir-fry counts.
4. Writing three lines in the log above. Three lines is nothing. Three lines a week is a journal.

## Next week

The forecast says sun, which means obligations return: a seminar I have to present at, a bike tune-up, and a promise to help a friend move a couch down two flights of stairs (no elevator, of course, this is America). I am not dreading it, exactly. But I caught myself checking the radar app twice today, hoping for another gray week, and I think that is the closest thing to a compliment I can pay the rain.

More soon. Probably about the couch.
