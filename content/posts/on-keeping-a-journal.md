I have kept some form of journal for nine years. Almost none of it is interesting, which
used to bother me.

## The early version was a performance

For the first two years I wrote for an imaginary audience. Every entry had a thesis.
Every day had a lesson. It was exhausting and, worse, it was inaccurate — the days that
didn't produce a lesson got skipped, and eventually most days didn't produce a lesson.

## What changed

I lowered the bar until I could not fail to clear it. Some days that means one line:

> Wednesday. Finished the draft. Tired but the good kind of tired.

That is a complete entry. Nine words, no thesis.

The rules I have now:

1. Never write for an audience, real or imagined.
2. Never require a lesson.
3. Keep the boring details — weather, food, who I talked to. They turn out to be the
   details that trigger memory later.
4. Write at the same time of day, not for the same length of time.
5. Do not read old entries while upset.

Rule 3 is the one people skip and the one that pays off most. "A difficult meeting" is
useless in five years. "A difficult meeting about the Q3 numbers, and I said the thing
about the invoice" brings the whole afternoon back.

## What it is actually for

Two things, neither of which is a record.

**It is a filter.** Writing forces a slow pass over a fast day. Things that seemed large
at 10 a.m. are usually small by 10 p.m., and occasionally the reverse — a throwaway
sentence turns out to be a real problem I had been avoiding noticing.

**It is evidence.** My memory of myself is a story I edit constantly. A journal is
occasionally wrong, but it is not self-serving in the same way. When I catch myself
thinking "I have always been like this", I can go check. Often I have been like this for
four months and like something else for nine years.

## The mechanics

Nothing exotic. One file per day, plain text, in a folder synced across devices. No app
with a subscription and no streak counter, because a streak turns a private habit into a
public obligation. Titles are just the date. Search is `grep`.

## The thing nobody warns you about

Reading an old entry from a difficult period is strange, because you remember the
situation but not the person you were in it — and that person made a lot of decisions
you can no longer reverse-engineer. There is nothing to do about that except write more
of it down while you are still them.

Nine years of it, and my favorite entries are still the boring ones.
