Notes from two weeks in Hefei in January, written mostly on my phone at odd hours.

## Arriving

The flight lands at 5 a.m. and the taxi smells like citrus air freshener and cigarettes,
which is how I know I am home. The driver asks if I am here for the holiday. I say yes.
He asks where I am from. I say here, and he laughs.

It is cold in the way that indoor cold is worse than outdoor cold. My grandmother's
apartment has heat but she does not use it, because in her view a person who is cold
should put on another sweater. I put on another sweater.

## Food

Everything I ate, ranked by how much I want it right now:

1. The dumplings from the stall by the market — thin skin, a lot of soup, scalding.
2. My grandmother's red-braised pork, which she insists is nothing special.
3. Breakfast noodles at 6:45 a.m. from the place with the plastic stools.
4. A very good, very cheap orange.

My grandmother watched me eat and said, at least four times, that I have gotten thin.
I have not gotten thin. This is not a fact-based opinion.

> "You are thin" is not information. It is a love language with an accuracy problem.

## The 7 a.m. bicycles

I walked every morning along the canal. At 7 a.m. there is a steady stream of bicycles
and scooters, all of them silent, because they are all electric now. When I was a kid
they were loud — two-stroke engines and actual bells. The street sounds completely
different and I couldn't decide how I felt about it.

At 7:20 the dancers arrive in the small plaza: eight women, one speaker, one routine they
have clearly been doing for years. Nobody watches them except me, and I watch them every
day for a week.

## Small observations

- Everyone's phone is bigger than everyone's hand.
- The convenience store clerk sleeps sitting up, and wakes up instantly, fully
  professional, as if he had not been asleep.
- My aunt asks me about my job in the same tone she asks about the weather.
- There is a new high-speed rail station, and the old station, and nobody will explain
  to me which one my ticket is for.

## Leaving

My grandmother packed me food I did not ask for and could not refuse: dried fruit,
tea, a bag of oranges, and a plastic bag of something I did not identify until I got
home (it was peanuts, salted, very good).

At security she stood outside the glass and waved until I turned the corner, which is
the correct way to do it.

I will come back in the summer.
