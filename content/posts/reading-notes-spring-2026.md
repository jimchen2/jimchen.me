Short notes on what I read between March and May. No reviews, just what stuck.

## Finished, and glad I did

**The Practice of Groundedness** — I expected a productivity book and got something
quieter. The argument that *consistency beats intensity* is not new, but it landed
differently at 11 p.m. on a Tuesday.

**A Pattern Language** — I read maybe a third of it and that was the right amount. The
idea that a good room has more than one source of light has permanently changed how I
look at apartments.

**The Changeling** — Strange, patient, occasionally infuriating. I finished it out of
spite and then thought about it for two weeks, which I suppose means it won.

## Started, abandoned, no regrets

| Book | Where I stopped | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A 900-page history of Byzantium | 22% | I could not hold the family trees in my head |
| A startup memoir | Chapter 3 | Every chapter was the same chapter |
| A physics popularization | Halfway | Beautiful, but I never retained anything |

Abandoning books used to feel like failure. Now it mostly feels like scheduling.

## Quotes I copied out

> The opposite of deep work is not shallow work; it is scattered work. Scattered work
> is indistinguishable from rest until you try to remember what you did.

> Everything is interesting if you go deep enough, and nothing is interesting if you
> only go an inch deep in a hundred directions.
